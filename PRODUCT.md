# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Radar4Flood serves public visitors, academic collaborators, university staff, project partners, interested agencies, and non-specialist readers who need a clear view of the project and its public-facing work. Visitors may arrive from institutional links, project updates, publication references, or the forecast entry point. They need to understand what Radar4Flood is, see evidence that the work is active and credible, find publications or team information, and reach the existing forecast webpage without mistaking this site for the forecasting interface itself.

## Product Purpose

Radar4Flood is a public academic website for a radar-based flood forecasting project. It introduces the project, publishes image-rich news and work updates, presents publications and staff information, provides basic contact details, and links clearly to the existing forecast webpage. Success means visitors can quickly understand the project, trust its academic and practical basis, browse current activity, and find the appropriate next destination in Thai or English.

## Brand Personality

The brand should feel credible: academically grounded, calm, precise, and accessible to readers outside the technical team. The voice should be professional and practical, avoiding exaggerated marketing claims while still making the project feel active, modern, and approachable.

## Anti-references

Radar4Flood should not look like a generic university template, a startup landing page with inflated claims, an internal monitoring dashboard, or a loud data visualization product. Avoid neon dashboard styling, heavy border systems, public login patterns, complex app-shell behavior, contact forms by default, and designs that suggest the forecast interface is being rebuilt inside this site.

## Design Principles

1. Lead with public clarity: explain the project and forecast entry point in language that non-specialists can follow.
2. Make credibility visible: foreground academic context, publications, field work, staff, and structured updates.
3. Keep the experience calm: use hierarchy, spacing, and restrained signal-led accents instead of dashboard noise.
4. Support both languages from the start: Thai is the default, with English routes and content handled as a first-class path.
5. Preserve a clean content workflow: Sanity-managed content should stay simple for staff editors, with typed local data only as a temporary bridge.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA. Prioritize strong contrast, semantic HTML, meaningful heading order, descriptive links, image alt text, keyboard navigation, reduced-motion support, and readable Thai-English typography. Long-form content should stay left-aligned with comfortable line length, and fallback behavior should be clear when English content is missing.
